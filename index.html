🔐 Word Vault

A vocabulary game disguised as safe-cracking. Five confusable words, one sentence with a hole in it — pick the word that truly fits, five in a row, and the vault swings open.

All five words fit the sentence's grammar — only one fits its meaning. The clue is in the reading: worn out, slowly, the whole team together. Every correct word charges one energy pin on the holographic lock; one wrong word — or letting the 15-second lock timer burn out — and the charge arcs away. A safe grants three runs, then it keeps its secret, shows you the words that got away, and the school year's run of pins starts again.

Get one wrong and it teaches instead of scolding: the word that fits and the word you picked appear side by side with their dictionary meanings, the cue, and one line on the difference. Cracked safes come back later as little review lockboxes with fresh sentences, so the words stick.

It adapts by itself

Levels follow Australian school years 1–7, pitched for keen and gifted readers. Within every year the vaults grow from 2 pins to the full 5, one step per clean crack: four clean cracks in a row pass the year. A miss starts the year's run again, and only a second stuck safe in a row steps back a year, so the game settles where the reader is challenged but never stuck. A grown-up can pick the starting year in Settings. Early years use everyday words with clearly different jobs (push / pull / lift / carry / throw) in short sentences; the top years separate words one shade apart (fleeting / intermittent) and the mix-ups school actually marks down: their / there / they're, borrow / lend. The lock timer keeps eyes on the cue (15 seconds a sentence, 20 in Years 1–2, and Settings can switch it off for slower readers). Every crack pays a collectible into the Curio Cabinet, and finished shelves unlock new looks for the vault.

Spelling, dictionary and word-forms vaults

Two more games use the same lock. The ✏️ Spelling Vault is a spelling test: hear the word, read its sentence, type it, and a miss shows exactly which letters differ. The 📖 Dictionary Vault matches words to their definitions, both ways, and the five definitions are always close cousins, so it rewards careful reading. The 🌱 Word Forms Vault puts versions of one word on the tiles (terrify / terrified / terrifying / terror) and asks which one the sentence needs. Each game keeps its own school year, and four clean cracks in a row pass it.

✅ Safe & private. No accounts, no ads, no chat, no purchases. The game makes no internet connection at all while playing: progress is saved only in this browser, on this device, and nothing is ever sent anywhere. One switch resets it for a new player.
